\begin{abstract}
Differentiable digital signal processing enables unsupervised estimation of sound processor parameters via analysis by synthesis. However, it relies on waveform and spectral pointwise losses that do not encode energy displacement, needed to obtain essential parameters like onset time and pitch. In this work, we propose \glspl{cel}, which compare \acrshort{stft} power accumulated in time and frequency directions. We use them to predict the pitch and timing of monophonic plucked-string musical phrases synthesised by a differentiable digital waveguide whose delay length defines pitch. In musical phrases containing one to eight notes, \glspl{cel} give the strongest gradient alignment with respect to pitch, timing and timbre, near exact estimation for 1 and 2 notes and lowest median pitch and time error on phrases with more than 2 notes with respect to previous waveform, spectral and optimal transport baselines. Still, it is comparable in runtime and memory usage to a single scale STFT loss. We believe these losses will further support unsupervised joint optimisation of pitch, timing and timbre, and provide a pip installable PyTorch package to be used in future work\footnote{The Ce$\mathcal L$s library is available at \url{https://github.com/ptablasdpaula/ICASSP27-Phrase/tree/cels-v0.1.0}.}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\glsresetall
Engel et al. coined the term \Gls{ddsp}, as the process of embedding audio processors in automatic differentiation graphs for efficient and interpretable unsupervised analysis by synthesis~\cite{engel2020ddsp}. The purpose is usually sound matching: retrieving the synthesiser parameters that would best synthesise the target sound. Since then, it has been widely adopted in multitude of applications, such as neural waveshaping, FM synthesis, reverberation, vocal tract modelling and effects modulation~\cite{hayes2021waveshaping, caspe2022ddx7, dalsanto2023diffdn, sudholt2023vocal, yu2024torchlpc, mitcheltree2025modulation, carson2026gradient}; for an early comprehensive review, we redirect the reader to Hayes et al.~\cite{hayes2024review}.

Still, most of the \gls{ddsp} literature predicts controls as either a constant set of parameters, or dense frame trajectories. These naturally fit impulse response or continuously driven sounds (\textit{e.g.} reverb and bowing respectively), but it is not how many instruments are instinctively performed. Previously, we proposed an event-set model to predict guitar notes~\cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus} with onset, pitch ($f_0$) and velocity among other physical parameters. This work expands this framework with the purpose of joining common \gls{mir} representations, where plucked string is represented as MIDI, sheet music or guitar tablatures~\cite{riley2024gaps,loth2025goat,loth2025guitarflow} with \gls{ddsp}.

However, $f_0$ and onset time represent a displacement of energy, which is notoriously difficult to optimise with the common waveform and pointwise spectral losses used in ~\gls{ddsp}, prone to oscillatory local minima and useless when there is no energy overlap~\cite{wang2019nsf,turian2020loss}. Recent revisions of \gls{mss} aimed to reduce the former issue by reducing spectral leaking~\cite{schwar2023mss}, but do not address the latter. Complex surrogates were proposed but remain synthesiser-dependant~\cite{hayes2023sinusoidal}. The literature commonly eludes this problematic via external detectors or synthetic labels, the first inherit the detectors' errors and veto further end-to-end training of $f_0$~\cite{engel2020inverse,engel2020ddsp,caspe2022ddx7,sudholt2023vocal}, while the latter generalises poorly to real-world data~\cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}.

Recent years have seen the adoption of \gls{ot}, which computes the minimum cost required to transport energy, in machine learning~\cite{monge1781deblais,cuturi2013sinkhorn,peyre2019computational} and audio applications, such as music transcription and source separation~\cite{flamary2016optimal,rolet2018blind}. Torres et al. applied it to \gls{ddsp} for harmonic parameter estimation as a frame-wise spectral $\mathcal{W}_2$, \gls{sot}~\cite{torres2024sot}; Fabiani et al. then proposed a joint time--frequency \gls{ot} similarity metric, \gls{tfw}~\cite{fabiani2024jtfot}. While promising, we showed that \gls{sot} is unreliable for joint onset--$f_0$ optimisation, and proposed \gls{tfw} as future work~\cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. Hence, we propose here a new family of losses: \glspl{cel}, which compare \gls{stft} power accumulation in time and frequency bins to predict plucked string musical phrases via optimisation of $f_0$ and timing of notes synthesised by a \gls{dwg}~\cite{smith2010physical}. We find \glspl{cel} are as or more efficient, yet perform better than all tested baselines.

Hence, our contributions are: (i) ~\glspl{cel}: an efficient family of losses that provide helpful gradients for pitch, onset time and timbre parameters by accumulating spectral power, which we provide as a pip installable PyTorch package. (ii) An unsupervised method based on \glspl{dwg} and ~\glspl{cel} to predict plucked string monophonic musical phrases.

\section{Cumulative Energy Losses}
\label{sec:losses}

We propose \Glspl{cel}, which expose energy displacement by comparing
accumulated spectral power. For target and candidate waveforms $x,\hat x$, let
$P=\Phi(x),\hat P=\Phi(\hat x)\in\mathbb R_+^{K\times N}$, where
$\Phi(x)_{k,n}=|\operatorname{STFT}(x)_{k,n}|^2$. We use a periodic Hann
window, a 1024-point \gls{fft}, and a 256-sample hop.

\newcommand{\dvec}{\textbf{\textit{d}}}
\newcommand{\dt}{d_\mathrm{t}}
\newcommand{\df}{d_\mathrm{f}}
\newcommand{\ttype}{\mathrm{t}}
\newcommand{\ftype}{\mathrm{f}}

A direction pair $\dvec=(\dt, \df)$ indexes one of four cumulative views. In time,
$\rightarrow$ includes frames $0,\ldots,n$ and $\leftarrow$ includes
$n,\ldots,N-1$; in frequency, $\uparrow$ includes bins $0,\ldots,k$ and
$\downarrow$ includes $k,\ldots,K-1$. Thus, the arrows give the starting corner
and accumulation direction. Writing $a\preceq_\dvec b$ for
$a\leq b$ when $\dvec\in\{\rightarrow,\uparrow\}$ and $a\geq b$ otherwise, the target and candidate surfaces are:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
S_\dvec(P)_{k,n}=\sum_{i\preceq_{\df}k}\sum_{j\preceq_{\dt}n}P_{i,j},\\
S_\dvec = S_\dvec(P), \quad \hat S_{\dvec}=S_{\dvec}(\hat P).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
Each value contains the power in the rectangle from the selected corner to
$(k,n)$, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:cumulative-surfaces}. Let
$m=\sum_{k,n}P_{k,n}>0$ and define the discrete random frequency--time location
$(\mathsf F,\mathsf T)$ by $\Pr(\mathsf F=\ftype_k,\mathsf T=\ttype_n)=P_{k,n}/m$.
Then $S_{\dvec}/m$ gives four quadrant combinations of \glspl{cdf} and cumulative
tails~\cite{torres2024sot,peyre2019computational}; for example,
$S_{(\rightarrow,\uparrow)}(P)_{k,n}/m
=\Pr(\mathsf F\leq \ftype_k,\mathsf T\leq \ttype_n)$. When candidate power equals $m$,
$\hat S_{\dvec}/m$ is analogous; otherwise, the common scale retains total-power
error. Their uniform average is invariant to reversal of either axis.

For a pointwise feature map $\phi:\mathbb R_+\rightarrow\mathbb R$, let
$E_\dvec$ be the candidate-minus-target surface error matrix. For any nonempty set
$\mathcal D$ of direction pairs, define
\begin{gather*}
E_\dvec = \phi(\hat S_{\dvec}/m)-\phi(S_{\dvec}/m),\\
\ell_\dvec = \frac{1}{\sqrt{KN}} \|E_\dvec\|_\mathrm{F}, \qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathcal D}(x,\hat x) = \frac1{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{\dvec\in\mathcal D}\ell_{\dvec}.
\end{gather*}

where $\|\cdot\|_\mathrm{F}$ denotes the Frobenius norm, normalising across $K$ frequency bins and $N$ time frames. We note that when $\phi(u)=u$ and accumulation is restricted to a single axis, our metric $\ell_{\uparrow}$ is exactly proportional to the discrete Cram\'er distance, defined as the $L_2$ norm of the CDF residual~\cite{szekely2013energy}. Extending this, frequency-only and time-only accumulations yield the scaled \gls{rms} of per-slice Cram\'er distances, while our diagonal accumulations extend this logic to evaluate two-dimensional quadrant-CDF discrepancies. Furthermore, the reader might note that applying an $L_1$ norm rather than $L_2$ to these same residuals would yield the one-dimensional $1$-Wasserstein distance~\cite[Remark~2.30]{peyre2019computational}.


\section{Differentiable plucked string Musical Phrases}
\label{sec:renderer}
We use \Glspl{dwg} as a common and efficient choice for modelling plucked strings~\cite{smith2010physical,depaula2026waveguides}. Our model has two oppositely travelling rails, each split at the excitation point into nut-side and bridge-side delays of $D_1$ and $D_2$ samples, respectively, as depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:waveguide}. For ideal delays and phase-inverting terminations, $f_0=f_s/[2(D_1+D_2)]$; the implemented model also compensates for interpolation and loss-filter phase. The relative pluck position is $D_2/(D_1+D_2)=0.23$ from the bridge. Nut and bridge reflection gains are $-1$ and $-H_L(z)$, respectively, with loss filter $H_L(z)=g(1-a_1)/(1-a_1z^{-1})$, $g=0.99$, and $a_1=0.2$. Here $g$ controls the DC gain and increasing $a_1$ increases high-frequency attenuation, providing decay and damping controls. Output is the bridge-incident travelling velocity. Each new note excites a \gls{hrc} $e_0(t)=A_e[1-\cos(\pi t/T_e)]/2$ for $0\leq t<T_e$ and zero otherwise, with $A_e=0.8$ and $T_e=10$~ms. This results in a rich signal of evenly spaced harmonics with an $f_0$-dependant decay time (see~\cite[Fig.~3]{tablasdepaula2026karplus}). In turn it poses rugged loss landscapes w.r.t $f_0$ and time for SOT and other traditional losses as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:loss-sweeps}, motivating the use of \glspl{cel}.

For differentiable onset placement, we use a Fourier phase-ramp delay implemented with FLAMO~\cite{dalsanto2025flamo}. To place an onset by $\delta_o$ samples, we multiply the spectrum of $e_0$ by $e^{-j\omega\delta_o}$, where $\omega$ is angular frequency in radians per sample and $j^2=-1$. The excitation is zero-padded to $2.048$ times the signal length to reduce circular wrap-around~\cite[Ch.~7]{smith2007dft}. We constrain the DC and Nyquist bins to be real to preserve Hermitian symmetry~\cite[Ch.~7]{smith2007dft}. Shifted note spectra are summed before one inverse FFT, retaining the original signal length as the summed excitation.

For gradient-based $f_0$ optimisation, each path combines an integer delay
with an order-3 Thiran all-pass section, chosen for its unity-magnitude
frequency response and maximally flat group delay at
DC~\cite[p.~49]{laakso1996splitting}. We
iteratively adjust the delays to account for the all-pass and loss-filter phase
at $f_0$.

Finally, we collapse the waveguide algebraically into a rational transfer
function and evaluate it in direct form II as required for PhilTorch with its TorchLPC
backend for fast GPU backpropagation~\cite{yu2025philtorch,yu2024torchlpc}. For phrase synthesis, we reset the recurrence history at each incoming note's onset, which is needed to smooth the multi-pluck loss landscape; we hypothesise that removing interference with residual string vibration contributes to this effect.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textbf{Synthetic data.}
All experiments use the \Gls{dwg} from Sec.~\ref{sec:renderer} to synthesise 16kHz, 2s signals. An $M$-note phrase contains fundamental frequencies $f_0\in[80,320]$~Hz and onsets $t\in[0.2,1.8]$~s, with $M\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$. We use reproducibly scrambled Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) to stratify all target coordinates~\cite{mckay1979comparison}. Target onsets are separated by at least 50~ms, while candidates span the complete registered domain.

\textbf{Metrics.}
For an unordered prediction, the Hungarian method~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} selects the note permutation $\pi$ minimising $\sum_i\{[\log_2(\hat f_{0,i}/f_{0,\pi(i)})]^2+ (\hat t_i-t_{\pi(i)})^2\}$, so one octave and one second have equal assignment cost. This assignment supplies the within-phrase pitch and onset mean absolute errors, reported in cents and milliseconds, respectively. It also reorders the target for our phrase-wide gradient cosine. We express each note as $(\log_2(f_{0,i}/80),t_i)$, concatenate these octave--second pairs into $\boldsymbol\theta$, and measure the cosine between the descent $\mathbf d=-\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\mathcal L$ and $\boldsymbol\theta^\star_\pi-\boldsymbol\theta$. The latter is the negative gradient of $\tfrac12\lVert\boldsymbol\theta- \boldsymbol\theta^\star_\pi\rVert_2^2$, so this scale-independent cosine measures agreement between the loss descent and the steepest descent of assigned parameter error; $1$, $0$, and $-1$ indicate descent directly towards, orthogonal to, and away from the assigned target. We report its mean and sample standard deviation across candidate phrases. As an objective independent audio metric, \gls{lsd} is the \gls{rms} difference between periodic-Hann \acrshort{fft}-1024/hop-256 log-magnitude spectra, using a common target-relative $-100$-dB magnitude floor.

\textbf{Baselines.}
Alongside waveform $L_1$ and $L_2$, single-scale STFT (SS) uses periodic-Hann
\acrshort{fft}-1024/hop-256 magnitudes. MSS sums linear- and log-magnitude
$L_1$ terms over periodic-Hann FFT sizes 2048, 1024, 512, 256, 128, and 64
with quarter-length hops, end padding, and no centring~\cite{wang2019nsf,engel2020ddsp}.
SmoMSS uses the flat-top window/hop pairs 67/32, 127/63, 257/128, 509/254,
1021/510, and 2053/1026 from~\cite{schwar2023mss}. \Gls{sot} uses flat-top
\acrshort{fft}-2048/hop-256 \gls{ot} per STFT frame and its $0.05$-weighted linear MSS
component; SOT-NC removes its asymmetric cumulative-mass cutoff and normalises
the two spectra independently~\cite{torres2024sot}. Both \gls{tfw} variants use
globally normalised periodic-Hann \acrshort{fft}-1024/hop-512 magnitudes and compute Sliced OT via ten projections~\cite{fabiani2024jtfot}. We adapt their $1$~s${}=1000$~Hz transport cost to log scale ($1$~s${}=1$~octave, log\gls{tfw}).

\textbf{CeL variants.}
The base Ce$\mathcal L$ averages all four diagonal direction pairs, uses
$\phi(u)=\sqrt{u}$, and applies a uniform \gls{rms}. logCe$\mathcal L$ instead
uses Log-Weighing: octave and time-bin widths weight the final \gls{rms}, so
equal octave spans receive equal total frequency weight while the cumulative
surfaces remain unchanged. decCe$\mathcal L$ retains uniform \gls{rms} but
makes accumulation local. It weights each STFT bin $P_{i,j}$ in $S_\dvec(P)_{k,n}$ 
by $w(\Delta t; 1\,\mathrm{s}) w(\Delta f; 1000\,\mathrm{Hz})$, where $\Delta t$ 
and $\Delta f$ are time and frequency separations between $(i,j)$ and $(k,n)$. 
The decay weight $w(r; H) = 1-\log_{10}(1+9r/H)$ for $r \le H$ (and zero for $r > H$) 
drops smoothly from $1$ at zero separation to $0$ at cutoff distance $H$. Finally, tlogCe$\mathcal L$ applies
Log-Weighing to only the two forward-time pairs
$(\rightarrow,\uparrow)$ and $(\rightarrow,\downarrow)$.

\subsection{Gradient Analysis}
\label{sec:gradient-diagnostic}
We ask how closely each loss's descent direction aligns with the displacement
that predicts a target phrase, before taking an optimiser step. For each $M$,
we generate 32 target phrases and 256 candidates per target from
independently scrambled joint LHS designs. The \emph{Joint} condition retains
both perturbations; \emph{Known time} restores target onsets and varies only
pitch; \emph{Known $f_0$} restores target pitches and varies only onset. Joint
is tested at $M\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$ and both conditional cases at
$M\in\{1,2,4\}$. Two further columns test reset candidates against persistent-state
$M=2$ targets, and vary all seven $M=1$ controls ($f_0$, onset, \acrshort{hrc}
amplitude and duration, pluck position, $g$, and $a_1$) scaled to $[0,1]$.
Thus, every condition contains 8192 candidate--target pairs, giving 106,496
pairs per loss overall, whose cosine alignment we report at \ref{fig:gradient-alignment}.

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced \acrlongpl{cel} (Ce$\mathcal L$s), which compare accumulated short-time Fourier transform (STFT) power in time and frequency and applied it to joint onset and $f_0$ optimisation in monophonic plucked string phrases with $M\in{1,2,4,6,8}$ notes. Across 106,496 phrase comparisons, Ce$\mathcal L$s achieve higher mean phrase-wide gradient cosine than waveform, spectral, and optimal transport objectives, although alignment falls as the number of notes $M$ grows (Fig.~\ref{fig:gradient-alignment}). Albeit \glspl{cel} remain faster and more memory efficient than \gls{mss} and \gls{tfw} (see Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}), note estimation is nearly exact at $M\in\{1,2\}$, while an energy decay variant gives the lowest median pitch, onset error and mean LSD at every $M$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lsd}). The gain from decaying accumulation suggests that localising displacement helps as phrases grow, motivating comparison with objectives that relax global mass transport, such as unbalanced optimal transport~\cite{sejourne2023unbalanced}.

Phrase parameter estimation remains limited to synthetic monophonic phrases with known $M$, fixed timbre, and waveguide state reset at each pluck. Real-world estimation would require jointly matching timbre alongside pitch and timing, while estimating the number of notes. Regarding the former, \glspl{cel} also retain the strongest alignment for persistent-state targets and when all seven synthesis controls vary, suggesting generalizability for sound matching in real-world data. The latter would require discrete optimisation, which we do not address. We note that while this work has focused on STFTs, alternative representations such as joint time--frequency scattering~\cite{anden2019joint} merit study given their use for related arpeggio-rate and pitch-range estimation~\cite{vahidi2023mesostructures}. Finally, we believe this work enhances DDSP by providing a method that should facilitate neural network training of pitch, timing and timbre parameters and provide code, reproducible experiments, and an installable PyTorch package for ease of use in future work.
