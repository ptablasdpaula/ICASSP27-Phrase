\begin{abstract}
Musical controls such as pluck time and fundamental frequency form a sparse
event sequence, but their phase-sensitive audio effects make them difficult
to recover by gradient descent. We study this geometry with a differentiable
two-rail digital waveguide and propose \lossBiCum, a bidirectional
cumulative-energy loss. \lossBiCum{} compares STFT power after cumulative sums in both directions of
time and frequency, turning a local energy displacement into an extended
difference. We compare six preregistered unweighted audio losses on 4500 direct
analysis-by-synthesis fits spanning five known event cardinalities, plus an
exploratory log-frequency-quadrature variant on 750 additional fits. Across 15
paired confirmatory tests, \lossBiCum{} has lower mean error than
\lossTFW{} in every comparison and survives familywise correction in 11. The
implementation includes signed manifests and fail-fast scientific invariants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Differentiable digital signal processing (DDSP) places interpretable audio
processors inside an automatic-differentiation graph \cite{engel2020ddsp} and
supports neural resynthesis systems such as DDX7 \cite{caspe2022ddx7}. The same
gradient can be used directly for label-free analysis-by-synthesis.

A pluck is naturally represented by onset and fundamental frequency. Both are
hard to fit: a small frequency error accumulates phase and produces repeated
local minima \cite{hayes2023sinusoidal}, while an onset error displaces energy
across analysis frames. Smooth multi-scale spectral loss reduces leakage
structure \cite{schwar2023mss}; spectral optimal transport moves energy rather
than comparing fixed bins \cite{torres2024sot}; and joint time--frequency
transport embeds both axes in its cost \cite{fabiani2024jtfot}. Related work
also shows that renderer and loss design jointly determine useful gradients
\cite{anonymous2026dafx,carson2026modulation}.

We isolate these effects with a fixed-cardinality phrase rendered by one
differentiable digital waveguide. Our contribution is \lossBiCum, a
bidirectional cumulative-energy objective, compared with waveform \lossLone,
waveform \lossLtwo, smooth \lossMSS, the published \lossSOT{} composite, and a
log-frequency time--frequency transport loss \lossTFW. All optimizing and
evaluation objectives in the registered rerun are unweighted. We additionally
test \lossLogQuad{}, an exploratory physically weighted variant of \lossBiCum.

\section{Differentiable phrase renderer}
Figure~\ref{fig:waveguide} shows the Waveguide renderer. It is a reciprocal
two-rail waveguide split at the excitation point, following established
plucked-string and digital-waveguide practice
\cite{valimaki1996plucked,depaula2026waveguides}. A rigid nut reflects with
gain $-1$, and the bridge reflection is $-H_L(z)$ with
\begin{equation}
H_L(z)=g\frac{1-a_1}{1-a_1z^{-1}},\qquad g=0.99,\quad a_1=0.2.
\end{equation}
The pluck position is 0.23 measured from the bridge. The output is the
bridge-incident travelling velocity; no pickup model is used.

We write a fractional delay as $\mathcal D_p(D;z)\approx z^{-D}$. A Thiran
all-pass preserves magnitude while approximating the requested group delay
\cite{laakso1996splitting}. The long pluck--nut segment uses order 3 and the
short pluck--bridge segment order 1 in both directions; the order is fixed at
construction and never selected from an event parameter. Rail tuning includes
the actual fractional-delay phase and one-pole loop-filter phase.

Each constant event regime is reduced algebraically to its exact rational
transfer. PhilTorch calls the native TorchLPC CUDA recurrence; a direct rail
recursion is retained only as a numerical oracle. A hard-reset phrase clears
all histories at each event boundary. The persistent diagnostic instead
carries the parameter-varying direct-form history. State policy is the only
difference between those two renderings.

Audio is float64, two seconds long, and sampled at 4 kHz. We first sample one
zero-onset half-raised-cosine (HRC) prototype
\begin{equation}
e_0(t)=\begin{cases}
0.4[1-\cos(\pi t/0.01)],&0\leq t<0.01,\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with amplitude 0.8 and duration 10 ms. For every event, the official FLAMO
v0.2.18 \texttt{parallelDelay} frequency response $e^{-j\omega D}$ places a
copy at the real-valued delay $D=t_if_s$. We use a 262,144-point transform,
disable FLAMO's alias-decay envelope, sum the independently delayed event
spectra, invert once, and retain the first 8000 samples. We do not mask the
small periodic sinc pre-ringing: such masking would add a second,
onset-dependent operation. The state-switch boundary remains the detached
$\lfloor t_i f_s\rfloor$ sample. Thus ``Fourier+Thiran'' below means Fourier
placement of the HRC only and the registered order-3/order-1 causal Thiran
sections for waveguide propagation. We adopt this pairing because it gives the
most consistently target-directed \lossBiCum{} geometry in
Table~\ref{tab:interpolation-onset}, while Fig.~\ref{fig:landscapes} verifies
the resulting gradients for all six losses on the full registered lattice.
There is no dynamics parameter, source filter, output normalisation, or
in-loop DC blocker.

\section{Audio losses}
Let $x$ and $\hat x$ be target and candidate. \lossLone{} is mean absolute
sample error and \lossLtwo{} is mean squared sample error. \lossMSS{} is the
smooth multi-scale spectral loss \cite{schwar2023mss}, using periodic flat-top
windows $\{17,31,67,127,257,509\}$, hops $\{8,15,33,63,128,254\}$,
$\log(1+|\operatorname{STFT}|)$ features, and summed squared
$\mathcal{L}_2$ distance.

\lossSOT{} is the full published composite \cite{torres2024sot}: a flat-top
FFT-512/hop-64 transport term on squared magnitudes with its published
normalisation and quantile cutoff, plus 0.05 times its Hann-windowed
linear-magnitude MSS term. \lossTFW{} adapts joint time--frequency sliced
$W_2$ \cite{fabiani2024jtfot}: a periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-128 magnitude STFT
is globally unit-normalised and projected along ten fixed angles. Frequency is
mapped to $\log_2(\max(f,20)/20)$ with one octave per second time--pitch
balance; the original method used linear frequency.

\subsection{Bidirectional cumulative energy}
Let $P$ and $\hat P$ be periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-64 STFT power. For time
direction $d_t$ and frequency direction $d_f$,
\begin{equation}
S_{d_t,d_f}=C^f_{d_f}\!\left(C^t_{d_t}(P)\right),\qquad
\hat S_{d_t,d_f}=C^f_{d_f}\!\left(C^t_{d_t}(\hat P)\right).
\end{equation}
Each directional pair uses the target maximum $m=\max S_{d_t,d_f}$:
\begin{align}
R&=\sqrt{\max(S_{d_t,d_f}/m,10^{-12})},\\
\hat R&=\sqrt{\max(\hat S_{d_t,d_f}/m,10^{-12})},\\
\ell_{d_t,d_f}&=\operatorname{RMS}(R-\hat R).
\end{align}
\lossBiCum{} averages the four choices
$d_t\in\{\rightarrow,\leftarrow\}$ and
$d_f\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}$. Accumulation extends a local displacement
from the event to a boundary, offering a global direction in time and
frequency. The implementation extends Sebastian Schlecht's
\texttt{MatchCumulativeEnergy} in both directions of both axes.

Canonical \lossBiCum{} takes an unweighted RMS over the rectangular STFT-bin
lattice. The exploratory \lossLogQuad{} instead evaluates that final RMS by
quadrature in physical coordinates: seconds along time and
$\log_2[\max(f,20)/20]$ octaves along frequency. For directional surface
difference $E_{d_t,d_f}=\hat R-R$, it uses
\begin{equation}
\ell^{\rm LogQ}_{d_t,d_f}=\left[\frac{\sum_{k,n}
w^f_{d_f,k}w^t_{d_t,n}E_{d_t,d_f}(k,n)^2}
{\sum_{k,n}w^f_{d_f,k}w^t_{d_t,n}}\right]^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where each $w$ is the exact adjacent-coordinate interval, aligned with the
chosen cumulative direction. The four surfaces, target-maximum scaling, and
square-root transform are unchanged. No event coordinates, detectors, or
salience weights enter this construction.

\section{Experiments}
Master seed 2027 generates scrambled-Latin-hypercube targets at each
$C\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$. The confirmatory manifest preserves the original ten
targets exactly and appends 140 targets under the same registered bounds and
separation rules; the final analysis uses target indices 0--149. Pitches
are log-mapped to 80--320 Hz. Sorted target
onset latents guarantee the registered 50-ms separation:
\begin{equation}
t_i=0.2+v_{(i)}[1.6-(C-1)0.05]+0.05i,
\end{equation}
within 0.2--1.8 s. Workers receive target audio, checksum, and cardinality,
never target parameters.

Every candidate pitch starts at 160 Hz and candidate onsets start at
\begin{equation}
\hat t_i=0.2+(i+0.5)\frac{1.6}{C}.
\end{equation}
Thus $C=2$ starts at 0.6 and 1.4 s. Pitch and onset use independent
elementwise bounded logits. Candidate onsets have no ordering transform,
spacing constraint, canonical parameterisation, or ``Magic Clamp''; slots may
cross or coincide and are matched only after fitting.

Targets and candidates use this same Fourier-onset/Thiran-propagation renderer.
We use float64 Adam with learning rate 0.05, betas $(0.9,0.999)$, epsilon
$10^{-8}$, and no weight decay. Division by each fit's initial raw loss is
scalar optimizer conditioning only. A relative raw-loss decrease of $10^{-4}$
resets patience. After 100 evaluations without such improvement, the strict
best is restored, learning rate is multiplied by 0.3 down to $10^{-5}$, and
Adam moments are cleared. Optimization stops after 250 further evaluations or
3000 updates and retains the lowest raw-loss iterate.

At $n=150$, the preregistered experiment has $150\times6=900$ single-event
fits and $4\times150\times6=3600$ multi-event fits, for exactly 4500 fits.
The independently run \lossLogQuad{} extension contributes 750 fits on the
same frozen targets, giving 5250 fits and 35 conditions in the combined
descriptive report. It was specified after the six-loss study and is excluded
from its confirmatory test family. All objectives are unweighted. Hungarian assignment
minimises
\begin{equation}
d_{ij}=\left[\frac{\log_2(\hat f_i/f_j)}{2}\right]^2+
\left[\frac{\hat t_i-t_j}{1.6}\right]^2.
\end{equation}
We compute within-phrase mean absolute cents and onset milliseconds after
assignment, then report their median across phrases. Periodic-Hann
FFT-256/hop-64 log-spectral distance (LSD) supplies an objective-independent
audio summary, shown as its complete per-phrase distribution.

All generation, optimization, aggregation, and figure boundaries reject
wrong schemas/cardinalities, mixed source identities, wrong tensor shapes or
dtypes, invalid target spacing, changed initialization, constrained candidate
coordinates, and non-finite audio, loss, gradient, coordinate, or metric data.

A signed noncentral-$t$ calculation defines the confirmatory family: paired
\lossBiCum{} versus \lossTFW{} comparisons for cents, onset, and LSD at five
cardinalities, giving 15 two-sided primary tests. Bonferroni familywise
$\alpha=0.05$, target paired $d_z=0.35$, and 90\% power require 150 phrases per
cardinality (power 0.901 at $n=150$ and 0.899 at $n=149$). All 15 paired
two-sided tests used the exact shared phrases. \lossBiCum{} had the lower mean
in every comparison. After correction, all five LSD comparisons and both
pitch and onset comparisons at $C\in\{4,6,8\}$ rejected their nulls (11/15);
pitch and onset at $C\in\{1,2\}$ did not.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
Targets are synthetic; cardinality is known; and target and candidate share
excitation, damping, and waveguide structure. The 4-kHz rate restricts
bandwidth, Thiran sections approximate ideal propagation delay, the finite
Fourier onset shift has small periodic pre-ringing, and hard reset is an
abstraction. The confirmatory inference is restricted to the preregistered
\lossBiCum{}--\lossTFW{} family; the seven-loss ranks are descriptive.
Unknown cardinality, real recordings, dynamics, pickup position, decay,
vibrato, slides, polyphony, and instrument-specific claims remain outside the
present evidence.

We formulate phrase analysis as direct recovery of event pitch and onset and
introduce a loss that compares cumulative STFT power in both directions of
both axes. The signed 5250-fit study provides physical-unit errors and audio
distributions under one controlled renderer. Its powered paired analysis
supports canonical \lossBiCum{} over \lossTFW{} in 11 of 15 corrected tests,
while \lossLogQuad{} improves the descriptive median pitch and onset recovery
at four, six, and eight events and remains a hypothesis for independent study.
