\begin{abstract}
Differentiable digital signal processing enables interpretable, unsupervised recovery of sound processor parameters via analysis by synthesis. However, it relies on waveform and spectral pointwise losses that do not encode energy displacement, needed to obtain essential parameters like onset time and pitch. On this work, we propose the use of Cumulative Energy Losses, which compare STFT power accumulated in time and frequency directions. We use them to recover the onset and pitch of sparse plucks rendered by a differentiable digital waveguide whose delay length defines the pluck's pitch, in the process recovering musical phrases. In a single-pluck landscape, we find our proposed losses have gradients that point towards the target at 120 equally spaced coordinates. Across 6000 fits containing one to eight pluck phrase fits, our proposed losses improve joint onset--pitch recovery over traditional waveform, spectral pointwise and recently proposed optimal transport objectives. We believe these losses will further support unsupervised optimisation of onset timing, pitch and delay line length, and provide a PyTorch library for ease of use in future applications\footnote{\textcolor{red}{link to library should be here}}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Engel et al. coined the term Differentiable digital signal processing (\ddsp), as the process of embedding audio processors in automatic differentiation graphs for efficient and interpretable unsupervised analysis by synthesis~\cite{engel2020ddsp}. Since then, it has been widely adopted in multitude of applications, such as neural waveshaping, FM synthesis, reverberation, vocal tract modelling and effects modulation ~\cite{hayes2021waveshaping, caspe2022ddx7, dalsanto2023diffdn, sudholt2023vocal, yu2024torchlpc, mitcheltree2025modulation, carson2026gradient}; for an early comprehensive review, we redirect the reader to Hayes et al.~\cite{hayes2024review}. 

In DDSP, controls are mostly predicted as either a constant set of parameters, or dense frame trajectories, naturally fit for IR prediction or continuously driven sounds respectively. However, that is not how many instruments are instinctively performed. Previously, we proposed an event-set predictor for the recovery of sparse guitar plucks \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}, forming note events with onset, pitch ($f_0$) and velocity among other physical parameters. This work expands this framework with the purpose of joining common MIR representations, where plucked string is represented as MIDI, sheet music or guitar tablatures ~\cite{riley2024gaps, loth2025goat,loth2025guitarflow} with \ddsp.

However, $f_0$ and onset time, representing a displacement of energy, are notoriously difficult to predict. This is due to the fact that~\ddsp     relies on waveform and pointwise spectral losses, such as Multi-Scale Spectral loss \mss, prompt to oscillatory local minima and useless when there is no energy overlap \cite{wang2019nsf, turian2020loss}. Recent revisions of \mss aimed to reduce the former issue by reducing spectral leaking \cite{schwar2023mss}, but do not address the latter. Complex surrogates were proposed but remain synthesiser-dependant \cite{hayes2023sinusoidal}. The literature commonly eludes this problematic via external detectors or synthetic labels, the first inherit the detectors' errors and veto further end-to-end training of $f_0$ \cite{engel2020inverse, engel2020ddsp, caspe2022ddx7, sudholt2023vocal}, while the latter generalises poorly to out of domain data \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}.

Recent years have seen the adoption of Optimal Transport (OT), which measures the shortest energy displacement cost, in machine learning \cite{monge1781deblais,cuturi2013sinkhorn,peyre2019computational} and audio applications, such as music transcription and source separation \cite{flamary2016optimal, rolet2018blind}. Torres et al. were the first to apply it to \ddsp for harmonic parameter estimation, as a frame-wise spectral $\mathcal{W}_2$, Spectral OT (\sot) \cite{torres2024sot}; followingly, Fabiani et al. proposed a sliced formulation of a joint time--frequency OT as a similarity metric (\tfw) ~\cite{fabiani2024jtfot}. 

While promising, we showed that (\sot) is unreliable for joint onset--$f_0$ optimisation, and proposed (\tfw) as future work \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. Hence, we propose recovering musical phrases via optimisation of $f_0$ and onset time of plucks rendered by a simple digital waveguide \cite{smith2010physical}, comparing (\tfw) with a newly proposed family of losses, Cumulative Energy Losses (Ce$\mathcal{L}$s), which compare STFT power accumulation in time and frequency bins.

\section{Bidirectional cumulative-energy loss}
\label{sec:losses}
The bidirectional cumulative-energy loss, \bicul{}, compares STFT power
accumulated towards all four time--frequency corners. Displacement alters values
between the energy and each boundary, exposing early/late and low/high direction
without favouring a boundary. Let $P_{k,n}$ and $\hat P_{k,n}$ denote target and
candidate periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-64 power at frequency bin $k$ and frame $n$.
For $q=(d_t,d_f)$, $d_t\in\{\rightarrow,\leftarrow\}$ and
$d_f\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}$ select accumulation direction; $C^t_{d_t}$ and
$C^f_{d_f}$ are the corresponding cumulative sums:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
S_q&=C^f_{d_f}(C^t_{d_t}(P)),\\
\hat S_q&=C^f_{d_f}(C^t_{d_t}(\hat P)),\\
m_q&=\max S_q,\qquad R_q=\sqrt{\max(S_q/m_q,10^{-12})},\\
\hat R_q&=\sqrt{\max(\hat S_q/m_q,10^{-12})},\\
\ell_q&=\operatorname{RMS}(R_q-\hat R_q).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Here $S_q,\hat S_q$ are the target/candidate cumulative surfaces; $m_q$ is the
target maximum; $R_q,\hat R_q$ are target-normalised square roots floored at
$10^{-12}$; and $\ell_q$ is their root-mean-square (RMS) difference over
$(k,n)$. \bicul{} averages all four $\ell_q$ without event coordinates,
detectors, or salience weights.

\bicul{} is related to CDF-based Cram\'er distances: in one dimension, the
Cram\'er discrepancy integrates squared CDF
differences~\cite{szekely2013energy}. With equal-mass unit-normalised power, one
accumulation direction, and no square-root feature, $\ell_q$ becomes a discrete
axis-aligned two-dimensional Cram\'er-type discrepancy. Both sense displacement
without pointwise overlap because a shift changes the intervening cumulative
region. \bicul{} is not the standard distance: target-only normalisation admits
unequal candidate energy, square roots compress dominant regions, and four
orientations avoid privileging one boundary.

Phrase recovery additionally evaluates Log-Q \bicul. It retains the same STFT,
cumulative surfaces, target-maximum normalisation, and square-root features, but
replaces the final uniform-bin RMS. For
$E_q(k,n)=\hat R_q(k,n)-R_q(k,n)$, the candidate-minus-target surface error,
the directional loss becomes
\begin{equation*}
\ell^{\rm quad}_{q}=\left[\frac{\sum_{k,n}
w^f_{d_f,k}w^t_{d_t,n}E_q(k,n)^2}
{\sum_{k,n}w^f_{d_f,k}w^t_{d_t,n}}\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation*}
Here $w^f_{d_f,k}$ and $w^t_{d_t,n}$ are direction-aligned adjacent-bin widths
in octaves and seconds, respectively; the frequency coordinate is
$\log_2[\max(f,20)/20]$, whose 20-Hz floor gives a finite origin. Log-Q denotes
log-frequency quadrature, not a constant-Q transform. Both sums span $(k,n)$,
$\ell^{\rm quad}_q$ replaces $\ell_q$, and all other operations are unchanged.

\section{Differentiable phrase renderer}
\label{sec:renderer}
The phrase renderer is the reciprocal two-rail digital waveguide in
Fig.~\ref{fig:waveguide}. Each pluck launches oppositely travelling waves into
nut- and bridge-side paths. Their delay-length sum sets $f_0$, while their ratio
sets pluck position~\cite{depaula2026waveguides}, fixed at 0.23 from the bridge.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{\PaperRoot/figures/Waveguide.pdf}
\caption{Two-rail digital waveguide renderer. $D_1$ and $D_2$ are the one-way
nut- and bridge-side delays; blue and red denote right- and left-travelling
waves. Ignoring loop-filter phase, the round-trip delay is $2(D_1+D_2)$ samples
and $f_0=f_s/[2(D_1+D_2)]$; the implementation phase-corrects the segment
delays for $H_L(z)$.}
\label{fig:waveguide}
\end{figure}

Nut and bridge reflection gains are $-1$ and $-H_L(z)$, respectively, where
$H_L$ is the loss-filter transfer, $z$ the transform variable, and $z^{-1}$ a
one-sample delay:
\begin{equation*}
H_L(z)=g\frac{1-a_1}{1-a_1z^{-1}},\qquad g=0.99,\quad a_1=0.2.
\end{equation*}
$g$ is low-frequency round-trip gain and $a_1$ the one-pole coefficient
increasing high-frequency attenuation. They produce a complex, $f_0$-dependent
RT60~\cite[Fig.~3]{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. Output is bridge-incident
travelling velocity. All experiments use the fixed configuration described
below for both target and candidate.

Audio is float64, 2~s long, and sampled at 4~kHz. Each event excites a zero-onset
half-raised-cosine (HRC)
\begin{equation*}
e_0(t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{A_e}{2}[1-\cos(\pi t/T_e)],&0\leq t<T_e,\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
where $e_0(t)$ is amplitude at elapsed time $t$, $A_e=0.8$ its peak, and
$T_e=10$~ms its duration. To place an onset by $\delta_o$ samples, we multiply
the 262,144-point spectrum of $e_0$ by FLAMO's phase response
$e^{-j\omega\delta_o}$~\cite{dalsanto2025flamo}, where $\omega$ is angular
frequency in radians per sample and $j^2=-1$. Event spectra are accumulated,
the DC and Nyquist bins are constrained to be real so the implied negative
frequencies preserve Hermitian symmetry, and one inverse FFT produces the
summed excitation; its first 8000 samples form the rendered interval.

Continuous $f_0$ likewise requires real-valued rail delays: integer-only lines
quantise pitch and provide no useful $f_0$ gradient. We split the phase-corrected
one-way string delay at the fixed pluck position into a long nut-side path and a
short bridge-side path, then approximate them with maximally-flat Thiran
all-pass delays~\cite{laakso1996splitting}. Their respective orders are 3 and 1.
A causal, stable order-$M$ Thiran section requires delay greater than $M-1$;
over 80--320~Hz, the shortest bridge-side delay falls below the order-3 limit,
whereas order 1 remains valid. Iterative rail tuning accounts for all-pass and
loop-filter phase. Thus all reported experiments fix Fourier onset placement
and Thiran 3/1 propagation.\footnote{An auxiliary \bicul-only screen also covers
analytic, Lagrange-5, Fourier, and Thiran-1 onset placement and Linear,
Lagrange, Fourier, and Thiran propagation:
\url{https://ptablasdpaula.github.io/ICASSP27-Phrase/#renderer-screen}. It is
a sensitivity diagnostic, not the basis for the fixed renderer or an
objective-independent ranking.}

The Thiran rails, reflections, and termination are algebraically collapsed into
one event-conditioned rational transfer and evaluated in direct form II (DF2)
through PhilTorch and its TorchLPC backend~\cite{yu2025philtorch,yu2024torchlpc}.
At each detached event boundary $\lfloor t_i f_s\rfloor$, event-wise reset zeros
the recurrence history before the new excitation; the persistent diagnostic
instead carries it across events. All recursive campaign rendering runs on
NVIDIA GPUs.

\section{Experiments and Methodology}
\subsection{Loss Objectives}
\label{sec:loss-objectives}
We compare six base objectives. \lone{} is mean absolute and \ltwo{} mean
squared sample error. Wang et al. introduced multi-resolution spectral
comparison~\cite{wang2019nsf}; Engel et al. adopted it as \mss{} in
\ddsp{}~\cite{engel2020ddsp}, and Schw\"ar and M\"uller later revisited its
design~\cite{schwar2023mss}. Following the latter, \mss{} sums squared \ltwo{}
distances between
$\log(1+|\operatorname{STFT}|)$ magnitudes, where $|\cdot|$ is magnitude and
$\log$ the natural logarithm. Its prime-length flat-top windows
$\{17,31,67,127,257,509\}$ and hops $\{8,15,33,63,128,254\}$ limit leakage
without aligning DFT bins. Torres et al.'s \sot{}~\cite{torres2024sot}
combines frame-normalised FFT-512/hop-64 flat-top STFT-power transport with
their unbalanced quantile cutoff and $0.05$ Hann-windowed linear-magnitude MSS.
Fabiani et al.'s \tfw{}~\cite{fabiani2024jtfot} globally normalises a
periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-128 magnitude STFT and projects it along ten angles.
We retain their scaling $\widetilde t=(10^3\,\mathrm{Hz/s})t$, making 1~s
commensurate with 1000~Hz in the ground cost. Immediately after this canonical
configuration, Experiment II tests a $1\,\mathrm{s}=1\,\mathrm{oct}$ variant
that changes \tfw{} coordinates to
$\log_2[\max(f,20)/20]$ octaves and seconds; $f$ is bin-centre Hz and the
20-Hz floor gives a finite origin. The sixth base objective is
Sec.~\ref{sec:losses}'s \bicul; Experiment II additionally evaluates its Log-Q
variant defined there. Experiment I compares the six base objectives, while
Experiment II compares all eight configurations.

\subsection{Experiment I: Single-event Gradient Diagnostic}
\label{sec:gradient-diagnostic}
We use a centred 160-Hz, 1.0-s target and a target-inclusive 25-by-25 lattice.
Time is linear over $\pm0.8$~s and pitch logarithmic over $\pm1$ octave, so
this relative lattice spans the complete registered candidate ranges
0.2--1.8~s and 80--320~Hz. Surfaces are independently min--max normalised.
Unit descent directions $-\nabla\mathcal L$ are displayed on an 11-by-11
sublattice with the target omitted, leaving 120 directions. A direction is
target-directed when its dot product with the target displacement is positive
in the normalised onset--pitch coordinates.

Figure~\ref{fig:landscapes} shows the six loss surfaces and directions using
Sec.~\ref{sec:renderer}'s fixed Fourier--Thiran renderer. Target and candidate
share this configuration, isolating loss geometry from renderer mismatch. This
is one full-range diagnostic case, not evidence of target invariance or a
population estimate.

\subsection{Experiment II: Phrase Recovery}
Seed 2027 generates 150 scrambled-Latin-hypercube targets at each of one, two,
four, six, and eight events. Pitches are log-mapped to 80--320~Hz; onsets span
0.2--1.8~s with at least 50~ms separation.

All candidate pitches start at 160~Hz and onsets at equal-cell centres over
0.2--1.8~s; independent logits bound coordinates to those same onset and pitch
ranges. Adam uses learning rate 0.05, default moments/epsilon, and no weight
decay. Initial-loss division conditions step scale only; reported values remain
raw. A relative raw-loss decrease of $10^{-4}$ resets patience. At patience 100
and 200, we restore the strict best, multiply the learning rate by 0.3 (floor
$10^{-5}$), and clear optimiser state; we stop at patience 250 or 3000 updates
and retain the best. Thus 150 targets $\times$ 8 losses $\times$ 5 event
counts${}={}$6000 fits.

\includegraphics[width=0.88\columnwidth]{\PaperRoot/figures/lsd_violin.pdf}

Let $i$ index an unordered prediction and $j$ a target. Their assignment cost is
\begin{equation*}
d_{ij}=\left[\frac{\log_2(\hat f_{0,i}/f_{0,j})}{2}\right]^2+
\left[\frac{\hat t_i-t_j}{1.6}\right]^2.
\end{equation*}
Hats mark predictions; $f_0$ is in Hz and $t$ in seconds; 2 octaves and 1.6~s
are the full ranges, making $d_{ij}$ dimensionless. The Hungarian
method~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} finds the one-to-one pairing minimising
$\sum d_{ij}$. Table~\ref{tab:pilot-compact} reports its errors and
Fig.~\ref{fig:lsd} the audio-error distributions.

Across phrases, we report medians of within-phrase pitch MAE (cents) and onset
MAE (ms). Periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-64 LSD is objective-independent.

Post hoc, each phrase's \bicul-family score averages \bicul{} and Log-Q; its
\tfw-family score averages canonical \tfw{} and the $1\,\mathrm{s}=1\,\mathrm{oct}$
variant. For each count--metric cell, the paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank
test ranks the magnitudes of the 150 family-score differences and compares
positive with negative rank sums to assess whether the differences are centred
at zero~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual}. Five counts $\times$ three metrics give 15
$p$-values, jointly adjusted by Holm's procedure~\cite{holm1979simple}.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Experiment I: Single-event Gradient Diagnostic}
In the loss order \lone, \ltwo, \mss, \sot, \tfw, and \bicul, the fixed
renderer gives 60.0\%, 49.2\%, 42.5\%, 51.7\%, 75.8\%, and 100.0\%
target-directed descent, respectively. Thus \bicul{} is the only objective with
120/120 target-directed directions in this registered renderer and diagnostic
case; Fig.~\ref{fig:landscapes} does not imply a renderer-independent ranking.

\subsection{Experiment II: Phrase Recovery}
Table~\ref{tab:pilot-compact} and Fig.~\ref{fig:lsd} report all conditions and
LSD distributions. Both \bicul{} variants are essentially exact at one and two
events. A \bicul{} variant has the lowest median pitch error at every count and
onset error through four; published linear-Hz \tfw{} has the lowest onset error
at six and eight.

Across these 15 tests, median paired family differences favour \bicul{}; 14
have $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\leq0.0069$, with only six-event onset above 0.05
($-10.5$~ms; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.186$).

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced \bicul{} for pluck-onset and pitch recovery. With fixed
Fourier--Thiran rendering, it gives 120/120 target-directed descents; reset
smooths the three-event DF2 sweeps. Across 6000 fits, \bicul{} strengthens joint
recovery, with Log-Q improving descriptive results. At four events, Log-Q
\bicul{}'s median pitch error (19.1 cents) is
comparable to reported JNDs~\cite{holmes2022pitch}, while its onset error
(6.75 ms) is below reported guitar-context timing JNDs~\cite{camara2026timing};
from six events, both errors exceed those perceptual scales, with pitch error
above one semitone. This contextual comparison does not establish perceptual
equivalence between JND experiments and phrase-level mean absolute errors.

Future work should test reset generality, expressive techniques, higher sample
rates, and persistent state.
