\begin{abstract}
\acrlong{ddsp} enables interpretable, unsupervised recovery of sound processor parameters via analysis by synthesis. However, it relies on waveform and spectral pointwise losses that do not encode energy displacement, needed to obtain essential parameters like onset time and pitch. In this work, we propose \glspl{cel}, which compare \acrshort{stft} power accumulated in time and frequency directions. We use them to recover the onset and pitch of sparse plucks rendered by a differentiable digital waveguide whose delay length defines pitch. Across 90,112 candidate--target comparisons containing one to eight events, \glspl{cel} give the strongest phrase-wide gradient alignment. We then compare the seven objectives whose single-event alignment rounds to at least .10, plus two low-alignment transport controls, across 6,750 musical-phrase fits. We believe these losses will further support unsupervised optimisation of onset timing, pitch, and delay-line length, and provide a PyTorch library for future applications\footnote{The Ce$\mathcal L$s library is available at \url{https://github.com/ptablasdpaula/ICASSP27-Phrase/tree/cels-v0.1.0}.}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\glsresetall
Engel et al. coined the term \Gls{ddsp}, as the process of embedding audio processors in automatic differentiation graphs for efficient and interpretable unsupervised analysis by synthesis~\cite{engel2020ddsp}. The purpose is usually sound matching: retrieving the synthesizer parameters, that would best synthesize the target sound. Since then, it has been widely adopted in multitude of applications, such as neural waveshaping, \gls{fm} synthesis, reverberation, vocal tract modelling and effects modulation ~\cite{hayes2021waveshaping, caspe2022ddx7, dalsanto2023diffdn, sudholt2023vocal, yu2024torchlpc, mitcheltree2025modulation, carson2026gradient}; for an early comprehensive review, we redirect the reader to Hayes et al.~\cite{hayes2024review}.

In most of the \gls{ddsp} literature, controls are predicted as either a constant set of parameters, or dense frame trajectories. These naturally fit \gls{ir} prediction or continuously driven sounds respectively. However, that is not how many instruments are instinctively performed. Previously, we proposed an event-set predictor for the recovery of sparse guitar plucks \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}, forming note events with onset, pitch ($f_0$) and velocity among other physical parameters. This work expands this framework with the purpose of joining common \gls{mir} representations, where plucked string is represented as \gls{midi}, sheet music or guitar tablatures ~\cite{riley2024gaps, loth2025goat,loth2025guitarflow} with \gls{ddsp}.

However, $f_0$ and onset time, representing a displacement of energy, are notoriously difficult to predict. This is due to the fact that~\gls{ddsp}     relies on waveform and pointwise spectral losses, such as \gls{mss}, prompt to oscillatory local minima and useless when there is no energy overlap \cite{wang2019nsf, turian2020loss}. Recent revisions of \gls{mss} aimed to reduce the former issue by reducing spectral leaking \cite{schwar2023mss}, but do not address the latter. Complex surrogates were proposed but remain synthesiser-dependant \cite{hayes2023sinusoidal}. The literature commonly eludes this problematic via external detectors or synthetic labels, the first inherit the detectors' errors and veto further end-to-end training of $f_0$ \cite{engel2020inverse, engel2020ddsp, caspe2022ddx7, sudholt2023vocal}, while the latter generalises poorly to out of domain data \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}.

Recent years have seen the adoption of \gls{ot}, which measures the shortest energy displacement cost, in machine learning \cite{monge1781deblais,cuturi2013sinkhorn,peyre2019computational} and audio applications, such as music transcription and source separation \cite{flamary2016optimal, rolet2018blind}. Torres et al. were the first to apply it to \gls{ddsp} for harmonic parameter estimation, as a frame-wise spectral $\mathcal{W}_2$, \gls{sot} \cite{torres2024sot}; followingly, Fabiani et al. proposed a sliced formulation of a joint time--frequency \gls{ot} as a similarity metric, the \gls{tfw} ~\cite{fabiani2024jtfot}.

While promising, we showed that \gls{sot} is unreliable for joint onset--$f_0$ optimisation, and proposed \gls{tfw} as future work \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. Hence, we propose recovering musical phrases via optimisation of $f_0$ and onset time of plucks rendered by a simple digital waveguide \cite{smith2010physical}, comparing \gls{tfw} with a newly proposed family of losses, \glspl{cel}, which compare \gls{stft} power accumulation in time and frequency bins.

\section{Cumulative Energy Losses}
\label{sec:losses}

We propose \Glspl{cel}, which expose energy displacement by comparing
accumulated spectral power. For target and candidate waveforms $x,\hat x$, let
$P=\Phi(x),\hat P=\Phi(\hat x)\in\mathbb R_+^{K\times N}$, where
$\Phi(x)_{k,n}=|\operatorname{STFT}(x)_{k,n}|^2$. We use a periodic Hann
window, a 1024-point \gls{fft}, and a 256-sample hop.

A direction pair $q=(d_t,d_f)$ indexes one of four cumulative views. In time,
$\rightarrow$ includes frames $0,\ldots,n$ and $\leftarrow$ includes
$n,\ldots,N-1$; in frequency, $\uparrow$ includes bins $0,\ldots,k$ and
$\downarrow$ includes $k,\ldots,K-1$. Thus, the arrows give the starting corner
and accumulation direction. Writing $a\preceq_d b$ for
$a\leq b$ when $d\in\{\rightarrow,\uparrow\}$ and $a\geq b$ otherwise, the
target and candidate surfaces are
\begin{equation*}
S_q(P)_{k,n}=\sum_{i\preceq_{d_f}k}\sum_{j\preceq_{d_t}n}P_{i,j},
\qquad \hat S_q=S_q(\hat P).
\end{equation*}
Each value contains the power in the rectangle from the selected corner to
$(k,n)$, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:cumulative-surfaces}. Let
$m=\sum_{k,n}P_{k,n}>0$ and define the discrete random frequency--time location
$(\mathsf F,\mathsf T)$ by $\Pr(\mathsf F=f_k,\mathsf T=t_n)=P_{k,n}/m$.
Then $S_q/m$ gives four quadrant combinations of \glspl{cdf} and cumulative
tails~\cite{torres2024sot,peyre2019computational}; for example,
$S_{(\rightarrow,\uparrow)}(P)_{k,n}/m
=\Pr(\mathsf F\leq f_k,\mathsf T\leq t_n)$. When candidate power equals $m$,
$\hat S_q/m$ is analogous; otherwise, the common scale retains total-power
error. Their uniform average is invariant to reversal of either axis.

For a pointwise feature map $\phi:\mathbb R_+\rightarrow\mathbb R$, let
$E_q$ be the candidate-minus-target surface error. For any nonempty set
$\mathcal Q$ of direction pairs, define
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
E_q(k,n)&=\phi(\hat S_q(k,n)/m)-\phi(S_q(k,n)/m),\\
\ell_q&=\left[\frac1{KN}\sum_{k,n}|E_q(k,n)|^2\right]^{1/2},\\
\mathcal L_{\mathcal Q}(x,\hat x)&=
\frac1{|\mathcal Q|}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\ell_q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\textbf{Relation to the Cram\'er Distance.}
For unit-mass $p,\hat p$ on $z_k=z_0+k\Delta$, let
$F_p$ be the CDF and write $F_p(k):=F_p(z_k)=\sum_{i\leq k}p_i$. One-axis
accumulation with $\phi(u)=u$ satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
d_{\rm C}^2(p,\hat p)&:=\int_{\mathbb R}|F_p(z)-F_{\hat p}(z)|^2\,dz\\
&=\Delta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}|F_p(k)-F_{\hat p}(k)|^2
=K\Delta\ell_{\uparrow}^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus $\ell_{\uparrow}=d_{\rm C}/\sqrt{K\Delta}$ exactly, with reverse
accumulation giving the same result. After unit normalisation of each slice,
frequency-only and time-only accumulation yield scaled RMSs of per-frame and
per-bin Cram\'er distances; diagonals instead yield two-dimensional
quadrant-CDF discrepancies. The one-dimensional $1$-Wasserstein distance
applies $L_1$ to the same CDF residual for which Cram\'er uses $L_2$
\cite{szekely2013energy}\cite[Remark~2.30]{peyre2019computational}.


\section{Differentiable Plucked-String Musical Phrases}
\label{sec:renderer}
We use \Glspl{dwg} as a common and efficient choice for modelling plucked
strings~\cite{smith2010physical,depaula2026waveguides}. Our model has two
oppositely travelling rails, each split at the excitation point into nut-side
and bridge-side delays of $D_1$ and $D_2$ samples, respectively, as depicted
in Fig.~\ref{fig:waveguide}. For ideal delays and phase-inverting terminations,
$f_0=f_s/[2(D_1+D_2)]$; the implemented model also compensates for
interpolation and loss-filter phase. The relative pluck position is
$D_2/(D_1+D_2)=0.23$ from the bridge.

Nut and bridge reflection gains are $-1$ and $-H_L(z)$, respectively, with
loss filter $H_L(z)=g(1-a_1)/(1-a_1z^{-1})$, $g=0.99$, and $a_1=0.2$.
Here $g$ controls the DC gain and increasing $a_1$ increases high-frequency
attenuation, providing decay and damping controls. The resulting decay times
depend on $f_0$, since the loss filter is traversed once per round trip,
as also illustrated for a related model in
\cite[Fig.~3]{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. Output is the bridge-incident
travelling velocity.

Each new event excites a \gls{hrc}
$e_0(t)=A_e[1-\cos(\pi t/T_e)]/2$ for $0\leq t<T_e$ and zero otherwise, with
$A_e=0.8$ and $T_e=10$~ms. For differentiable onset placement, we
use a Fourier phase-ramp delay implemented with FLAMO~\cite{dalsanto2025flamo}.
To place an onset by $\delta_o$ samples, we multiply the spectrum of $e_0$ by
$e^{-j\omega\delta_o}$, where $\omega$ is angular frequency in radians per
sample and $j^2=-1$. The excitation is zero-padded to $2.048$ times the signal
length to reduce circular wrap-around~\cite[Ch.~7]{smith2007dft}.
We constrain the DC and Nyquist bins to be real to preserve Hermitian
symmetry~\cite[Ch.~7]{smith2007dft}. Shifted event spectra are summed before
one inverse FFT, retaining the original signal length as the summed excitation.

For gradient-based $f_0$ optimisation, each path combines an integer delay
with an order-3 Thiran all-pass section, chosen for its unity-magnitude
frequency response and maximally flat group delay at
DC~\cite[p.~49]{laakso1996splitting}. We
iteratively adjust the delays to account for the all-pass and loss-filter phase
at $f_0$.

Finally, we collapse the waveguide algebraically into a rational transfer
function and evaluate it in \gls{df2} as required for PhilTorch with its TorchLPC
backend for fast GPU backpropagation~\cite{yu2025philtorch,yu2024torchlpc}. For phrase synthesis, we reset
the recurrence history at each incoming event's onset, rounded down to a
sample boundary and detached from differentiation. Resetting smooths the
multi-pluck loss landscapes in our diagnostic; we hypothesise that removing
interference with residual string vibration contributes to this effect.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textbf{Synthetic data.}
All experiments use the two-second, 16-kHz synthesis configuration in
Sec.~\ref{sec:renderer}. An $N$-event phrase contains fundamental frequencies
$f_0\in[80,320]$~Hz and onsets $t\in[0.2,1.8]$~s, with
$N\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$. We use reproducibly scrambled Latin hypercube sampling
(LHS) to stratify all target coordinates~\cite{mckay1979comparison}. Target onsets are separated by at
least 50~ms, while candidates span the complete registered domain.

\textbf{Metrics.}
For an unordered prediction, the Hungarian method~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian}
selects the event permutation $\pi$ minimising
$\sum_i\{[\log_2(\hat f_{0,i}/f_{0,\pi(i)})]^2+
(\hat t_i-t_{\pi(i)})^2\}$, so one octave and one second have equal assignment
cost. This assignment supplies the
within-phrase pitch and onset mean absolute errors, reported in cents and
milliseconds, respectively. It also reorders the target for our phrase-wide
gradient cosine. We express each event as
$(\log_2(f_{0,i}/80),t_i)$, concatenate these octave--second pairs into
$\boldsymbol\theta$, and measure the cosine between the descent
$\mathbf d=-\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\mathcal L$ and
$\boldsymbol\theta^\star_\pi-\boldsymbol\theta$. This scale-independent
alignment follows the use of gradient cosine similarity to identify helpful
directions~\cite{du2018gradient}; $1$, $0$, and $-1$ indicate descent directly
towards, orthogonal to, and away from the assigned target. We report its mean
and sample standard deviation across candidate phrases. As an
objective-independent audio metric, \gls{lsd} is the \gls{rms} difference
between periodic-Hann \acrshort{fft}-1024/hop-256 log-magnitude spectra, using a
common target-relative $-100$-dB magnitude floor.

\textbf{Baselines.}
Alongside waveform $L_1$ and $L_2$, we use a single-scale STFT (SS) using a periodic-Hann
\acrshort{fft}-1024/hop-256 magnitudes. MSS sums linear- and log-magnitude
$L_1$ terms over periodic-Hann FFT sizes 2048, 1024, 512, 256, 128, and 64,
with quarter-length hops, end padding, and no centring, as in~\cite{wang2019nsf,engel2020ddsp}.
SmoMSS uses the flat-top prime window/hop pairs 67/32, 127/63, 257/128, 509/254,
1021/510, and 2053/1026 proposed by ~\cite{schwar2023mss}. \Gls{sot} uses flat-top
\acrshort{fft}-2048/hop-256 transport and its $0.05$-weighted linear MSS
component; SOT-NC removes its asymmetric cumulative-mass cutoff and normalises
the two spectra independently~\cite{torres2024sot}. Both \gls{tfw} variants use
globally normalised periodic-Hann \acrshort{fft}-1024/hop-512 magnitudes and ten projections: the
published coordinates set $1$~s${}=1000$~Hz. Additionally, we propose a log-frequency variant where cost is $1$~s${}=1$~ (log\gls{tfw})\cite{fabiani2024jtfot}.

\textbf{CeL variants.}
The base Ce$\mathcal L$ averages all four diagonal direction pairs, uses
$\phi(u)=\sqrt{u}$, and applies a uniform \gls{rms}. logCe$\mathcal L$ instead
uses Log-Weighing: octave and time-bin widths weight the final \gls{rms}, so
equal octave spans receive equal total frequency weight while the cumulative
surfaces remain unchanged. decCe$\mathcal L$ retains uniform \gls{rms} but
makes accumulation local by multiplying contributions at separations
$\Delta t$ and $\Delta f$ by
$w(\Delta t;1\,\mathrm{s})w(\Delta f;1000\,\mathrm{Hz})$, where
$w(d;H)=\max\{0,1-\log(1+9d/H)/\log 10\}$. Finally, tlogCe$\mathcal L$ applies
Log-Weighing to only the two forward-time pairs
$(\rightarrow,\uparrow)$ and $(\rightarrow,\downarrow)$.

\subsection{Gradient Analysis}
\label{sec:gradient-diagnostic}
We ask how closely each loss's descent direction aligns with the displacement
that recovers a target phrase, before taking an optimiser step. For each
cardinality, we generate 32 target phrases and 256 candidates per target from
independently scrambled joint LHS designs. The \emph{Joint} condition retains
both perturbations; \emph{Known time} restores target onsets and varies only
pitch; \emph{Known $f_0$} restores target pitches and varies only onset. Joint
is tested at $N\in\{1,2,4,6,8\}$ and both conditional cases at
$N\in\{1,2,4\}$. Thus, every condition contains 8192 candidate--target pairs,
giving 90,112 pairs per loss overall. Hungarian matching remains unrestricted
in the conditional cases, and all gradient components are retained, so
movement in a known coordinate reduces alignment.

\subsection{Musical Phrase Recovery}
Seed 2029 generates 150 scrambled-LHS targets at each cardinality. Every
candidate begins with all pitches at 160~Hz and onsets isochronously spaced
within 0.2--1.8~s; independent logits constrain both coordinates to their
registered ranges. We retain the seven objectives whose one-event Joint mean
cosine rounds to at least $.10$. We additionally retain \gls{sot} and linear
\gls{tfw} as low-alignment controls, testing whether the diagnostic predicts
final optimisation.

Each of the resulting 6,750 fits uses Adam with learning rate $.05$. Division
by each phrase's initial loss conditions the step scale while leaving reported
losses raw. ReduceLROnPlateau uses factor $.5$ and patience 200. Independent
early stopping uses a 1000-update patience, with a 20,000-update cap. We report
each fit's lowest-loss iterate.

\section{Conclusion}
We have introduced a new family of losses \glspl{cel}, which compare STFT power accumulated in time and frequency directions and proposed its use alongside a Digital Waveguide for the recovery of monophonic plucked-string musical phrases, via optimisation of their independent pitch and onset times. Across 90,112 Latin Hypercube Sampled comparisons of musical phrases of increasing number of events (1 to 8), we find that \glspl{cel} preserve stronger cosine similarity for joint pitch-time optimisation than any other previously proposed waveform, spectral or optimal transport-based losses. However, as the number of events increases, cosine similarity also decreases substantially. Gradient descent optimisation aligns closely with these results, where our proposed \glspl{cel} beat all other losses at the task of musical phrase recovery, being near perfect for 1 and 2 events but progressively collapsing at increasing number of events, as can be seen both in \ref{fit} and \ref{fig:lsd}. As seen in the results, adding a progressive decay in the accumulation improves phrase-matching substantially for $N=4$ events, but collapses as $N$ increases. This suggests global energy displacement might not always be beneficial, directing our attention towards unbalanced variants of OT \cite{sejourne2023unbalanced}. Additionally, we note this work uses only STFT as a representation, where Joint Time Frequency Scattering has previously been proposed for the related task of arpeggio rate and pitch range prediction, and also deserve of careful attention \cite{anden2019joint, vahidi2023mesostructures}.

We point out, that the current approach has several limitations. Firstly, it addresses only monophonic synthetic data and needs of the filter state to be reset between plucks, corresponding to a hard mute before every new injection of energy - further research is needed to corroborate why persistence of energy between plucks complicates optimisation, and whether resetting is a viable surrogate technique for matching targets where this is not present. Extending this work to real-world data would require matching timbre as well as pitch and onset times, which this work does not address. Lastly we might not know the number of events occurring in a real-world performance. Predicting this variable introduces a non-differentiable categorical/binary difficult to address with gradient descent.

We believe this work introduces a viable method for the matching of event times and pitch, with potential impacts for unsupervised onset detection, and any other DDSP application where variables concerning displacement of energy are needed.
