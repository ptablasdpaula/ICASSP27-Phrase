\begin{abstract}
% REVIEW P1-22 [clarity]: language pass needed before this goes anywhere. In this paragraph: "we find out proposed" -> "our proposed"; "On this work" -> "In this work". Elsewhere: "synth dependant" (l.67), "aggresively" (l.69), "fugure work" (l.332), "collapse to incoherent phrases in most of the time" (l.329), missing comma after "As can be seen" (l.329).
Differentiable digital signal processing has become a framework adopted by numerous tasks, allowing unsupervised, interpretable, analysis by synthesis. Yet, essential parameters such as fundamental frequency and onset estimation remain difficult to recover. Traditional waveform and pointwise spectral losses do not encode the displacement direction caused by these parameters, prompting the use of external detectors. These bypass the problematic geometry but prevent the loss from correcting their estimates, causing a reconstruction ceiling. On this work, we compare traditional and previously proposed optimal transport based losses, and propose a new efficient loss based on cumulative energy, comparing spectral power accumulations in both directions of time and frequency. We use this loss to optimise a sparse sequence of plucks exciting a differentiable digital waveguide, whose delay length defines the fundamental frequency, to render musical phrases. Firstly, we find out proposed cumulative energy loss is the only objective whose gradients are directed towards the correct onset and fundamental frequency in a single pluck landscape. Secondly, we cross four onset placement methods and four waveguide fractional delay interpolators, and identify Fourier placement with Thiran propagation as the most consistently target directed combination. Lastly, across 6000 fits of musical phrases containing one to eight plucks, we find our proposed cumulative energy loss gives more reliable joint onset--pitch recovery than traditional and optimal transport objectives. These results support unsupervised optimisation of pitch, event timing, and delay-line length.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% REVIEW LATEX [latex]: plain space before \cite here and at l.67, 69, 125, 175, 246 — use a tilde so the citation number can never wrap alone onto the next line.
Differentiable digital signal processing (\ddsp) allows analysis by synthesis of audio processors within automatic differentiation graphs \cite{engel2020ddsp}, triggering its wide adoption in sound matching of multiple synthesizers and audio effects \cite{caspe2022ddx7,carson2026gradient}. However, it usually represents control as either global LTI processors, or dense frame rate trajectories. While the first naturally represent static systems, such as room reverberation \cite{dalsanto2025flamo}, the latter suits continuously driven instruments, such as bowing or singing voice \cite{sudholt2023vocal}. However, a range of instruments are not faithfully represented by either of these approaches; plucked and struck strings are, expressive techniques aside, instinctively performed as a collection of note events with onset, pitch, duration and velocity, as commonly represented in MIR and symbolic music \cite{hawthorne2018onsets}.

% REVIEW P1-10 [citation]: related work is confined to audio DDSP. The same problem — a non-convex misfit whose gradients point the wrong way under displacement, fixed by a cumulative or transport formulation — is solved and heavily cited in seismic full-waveform inversion. Sambridge et al. (2022) build a differentiable Wasserstein misfit from marginalised CDFs and report 77% vs 41% global convergence against L2, which is structurally the same experiment as Fig. 1. Not required for ICASSP, but one sentence makes the contribution look situated rather than local.
However, as Turian and Henry found, traditional pointwise and waveform-based losses are poor at pitch estimation, as they become misleading when spectral energy is displaced and does not overlap \cite{turian2020loss}. Hayes et al. proposed complex surrogates to sidestep this issue, but remain synth dependant \cite{hayes2023sinusoidal}. A common trick is the use of external $f_0$ detectors or synthetic parameter labels, capping reconstruction and vetoing further end-to-end training \cite{engel2020ddsp, engel2020inverse}. Optimal transport has progressively been introduced to DDSP by Torres et al. and Fabiani et al., as it addresses displacement directly, with STFT frame-wise and joint time-frequency implementations proposed for $f_0$ independently and jointly for time and frequency shifts respectively \cite{torres2024sot, fabiani2024jtfot}.

Tablas de Paula et al. recently proposed an event-based prediction architecture, jointly estimating the $f_0$, onset time and timbre parameters of events in a plucked-string algorithm \cite{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. \section{Cumulative energy losses}
\label{sec:losses}
The bidirectional cumulative-energy loss, \bicul{}, compares STFT power
accumulated towards all four time--frequency corners. Displacement alters values
between the energy and each boundary, exposing early/late and low/high direction
without favouring a boundary. Let $P_{k,n}$ and $\hat P_{k,n}$ denote target and
candidate periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-64 power at frequency bin $k$ and frame $n$.
For $q=(d_t,d_f)$, $d_t\in\{\rightarrow,\leftarrow\}$ and
$d_f\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}$ select accumulation direction; $C^t_{d_t}$ and
$C^f_{d_f}$ are the corresponding cumulative sums:
% REVIEW LATEX [latex]: this numbered equation carries no \label, as do those at l.179, l.191, l.284 and l.310 — each consumes a number nothing can reference. Meanwhile eq:pullback at l.168 is labelled and never referenced. Unnumber the ones you do not cite (equation*).
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_q&=C^f_{d_f}(C^t_{d_t}(P)),\\
\hat S_q&=C^f_{d_f}(C^t_{d_t}(\hat P)),\\
m_q&=\max S_q,\qquad R_q=\sqrt{\max(S_q/m_q,10^{-12})},\\
\hat R_q&=\sqrt{\max(\hat S_q/m_q,10^{-12})},\\
\ell_q&=\operatorname{RMS}(R_q-\hat R_q).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $S_q,\hat S_q$ are the target/candidate cumulative surfaces; $m_q$ is the
target maximum; $R_q,\hat R_q$ are target-normalised square roots floored at
$10^{-12}$; and $\ell_q$ is their root-mean-square (RMS) difference over
$(k,n)$. \bicul{} averages all four $\ell_q$ without event coordinates,
detectors, or salience weights.

\section{Differentiable phrase renderer}
\label{sec:renderer}
Let $x,\hat x\in\mathbb R^N$ be target and candidate $N$-sample waveforms,
$\theta$ the onset--pitch vector, $\rho$ a renderer realisation,
$\hat x=R_\rho(\theta)$ its output, and $\mathcal L$ the audio loss. Then
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\theta\mathcal L(x,R_\rho(\theta))=
J_{R_\rho}(\theta)^\mathsf{T}\nabla_{\hat x}\mathcal L(x,\hat x).
\label{eq:pullback}
\end{equation}
Here $J_{R_\rho}=\partial\hat x/\partial\theta$ is the renderer Jacobian;
$\nabla_u$ and $\mathsf T$ denote differentiation in $u$ and transposition.

Figure~\ref{fig:waveguide} shows $R_\rho$: two oppositely travelling rails split
by the pluck into nut- and bridge-side paths. Their length sum sets $f_0$ and
their ratio sets pluck position \cite{depaula2026waveguides}, fixed at 0.23
from the bridge. Nut and bridge reflection gains are $-1$ and $-H_L(z)$,
respectively, where $H_L$ is the loss-filter transfer, $z$ the transform
variable, and $z^{-1}$ a one-sample delay:
\begin{equation}
H_L(z)=g\frac{1-a_1}{1-a_1z^{-1}},\qquad g=0.99,\quad a_1=0.2.
\end{equation}
$g$ is low-frequency round-trip gain and $a_1$ the one-pole coefficient
increasing high-frequency attenuation. They produce a complex, $f_0$-dependent
RT60 \cite[Fig.~3]{tablasdepaula2026karplus}. Output is bridge-incident
travelling velocity. Thus Sec.~\ref{sec:losses} fixes $\rho$ to compare losses;
the remainder of this section fixes \bicul{} to compare renderer realisations.

\subsection{Differentiable onset placement and interpolation}
Audio is float64, 2~s long, and sampled at 4~kHz. Each event excites a zero-onset
half-raised-cosine (HRC)
\begin{equation}
e_0(t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{A_e}{2}[1-\cos(\pi t/T_e)],&0\leq t<T_e,\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $e_0(t)$ is amplitude at elapsed time $t$, $A_e=0.8$ its peak, and
$T_e=10$~ms its duration. Continuous placement uses analytic HRC sampling
(Naive); a six-tap, fifth-order maximally-flat FIR shift (Lagrange); the FLAMO

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Targets}
Seed 2027 generates 150 scrambled-Latin-hypercube targets at each of one, two,
four, six, and eight events. Pitches are log-mapped to 80--320~Hz; onsets span
0.2--1.8~s with at least 50~ms separation.

\subsection{Optimisation}
All candidate pitches start at 160~Hz and onsets at equal-cell centres over
0.2--1.8~s; independent logits bound coordinates to those same onset and pitch
ranges. Adam uses learning rate 0.05, default moments/epsilon, and no weight
decay; initial-loss division equalises numerical scale but not reported values.
A relative raw-loss decrease of $10^{-4}$ resets patience. At patience 100 and
200, we restore the strict best, multiply learning rate by 0.3 (floor
$10^{-5}$), and clear optimiser state. We stop at patience 250 or 3000 updates,
retaining the best iterate.

\subsection{Loss variants}
\label{sec:loss-variants}
The $1\,\mathrm{s}=1\,\mathrm{oct}$ variant changes \tfw{} coordinates to
$\log_2[\max(f,20)/20]$ octaves and seconds; $f$ is bin-centre Hz and the
20-Hz floor gives a finite origin. Log-Q changes only \bicul's final RMS from
uniform bins to seconds--log-frequency quadrature. For $q=(d_t,d_f)$, let
$E_q(k,n)=\hat R_q(k,n)-R_q(k,n)$ be the candidate-minus-target surface error.
Its weighted directional loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell^{\rm quad}_{q}=\left[\frac{\sum_{k,n}
w^f_{d_f,k}w^t_{d_t,n}E_q(k,n)^2}
{\sum_{k,n}w^f_{d_f,k}w^t_{d_t,n}}\right]^{1/2},

\subsection{Evaluation}
Let $i$ index an unordered prediction and $j$ a target. Their assignment cost is
\begin{equation}
d_{ij}=\left[\frac{\log_2(\hat f_{0,i}/f_{0,j})}{2}\right]^2+
\left[\frac{\hat t_i-t_j}{1.6}\right]^2.
\end{equation}
Hats denote predictions; $\hat f_{0,i},f_{0,j}$ are pitches in Hz;
$\hat t_i,t_j$ are onsets in seconds; $2$ and $1.6$ are the full ranges in
octaves and seconds; and $d_{ij}$ is dimensionless. Hungarian assignment finds
the one-to-one pairing minimising $\sum d_{ij}$. Table~\ref{tab:pilot-compact}
reports its errors and Fig.~\ref{fig:lsd} the audio-error distributions.

We report across-phrase medians of within-phrase mean absolute pitch cents and
onset milliseconds. Periodic-Hann FFT-256/hop-64 log-spectral distance (LSD)
gives an objective-independent audio metric.

We introduced direct recovery of pluck onset and pitch and \bicul{}, a bidirectional cumulative-STFT-power loss. Across 6000 fits, \bicul{} gave target-directed single-pluck gradients and stronger multi-pluck joint recovery than the comparison objectives; Log-Q further improved descriptive results. Fourier onset placement with Thiran propagation was the strongest renderer, and event-wise reset corrected persistent DF2 and Literal gradients. Although promising and better than other losses shown here, musical phrase recovery of 4 and more events is stronger than just-noticeable-difference and should be addressed in fugure work. 

Future work should address vibrato and other expressive techniques, higher sample rates and techniques that preserve a smooth loss landscape for onset time and $f_0$ without resetting the state of the waveguide. Although outside of the scope of this work, the question of whether a neural network is capable of escaping the local minima introduced in multi-pluck scenarios is open, and now fully testable thanks to this work.
