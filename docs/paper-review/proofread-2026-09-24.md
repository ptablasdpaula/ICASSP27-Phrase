## Remaining substantive points for author review

1. **Cramér and Wasserstein qualifications (Section II).** Restricting accumulation to one axis does not by itself turn globally normalised STFT slices into probability distributions. The direct 1D equivalence requires unit-mass (or equally normalised equal-mass) distributions and the relevant uniform-grid/RMS factors. The per-slice interpretation requires the same qualification for each slice; globally equal mass does not ensure equal mass in each frame. The L1/CDF statement likewise needs the 1D equal-mass and grid-width qualifications. Suggested concise opening: “For one-dimensional unit-mass distributions on a uniform grid and phi(u)=u, a single-direction loss is proportional to the Cramér distance, up to fixed grid and RMS factors.” The paragraph was left for a deliberate mathematical revision rather than silently changing its claims.
2. **Axis-reversal sentence (Section II).** “Their uniform average is invariant to reversal of either axis” is positioned among the cumulative surfaces. The useful invariant is the scalar loss averaged over all four directions, under simultaneous reversal of target and candidate. Reversal permutes the directional terms. Make this explicit after the loss definition; it is not a claim that individual cumulative surfaces stay pointwise unchanged.
3. **Log-Weighing terminology.** “Weighting” is the standard word for multiplying contributions by numerical weights, in both British and American English. “Weighing” normally refers to measuring weight or assessing something. Kept the user's chosen method name and table heading unchanged; recommend agreeing a global rename to “Log-Weighting”/“Weighting”.
4. **Real-world claims.** Persistent-state and seven-control tests remain synthetic gradient diagnostics. They motivate further work but do not establish real-recording recovery. “Suggesting generalisability ... in real-world data” should remain clearly tentative; a more precise formulation is “motivating tests on real recordings”. Similarly, estimating unknown cardinality does not universally require an explicitly discrete optimiser: continuous relaxations/model-selection approaches exist. These are scientific scope choices, not spelling fixes.
5. **Benchmark scope.** `scripts/benchmark_loss_efficiency.py` times render + target-bound loss + backward and a simple small gradient step, not the full Adam/scheduler loop used for recovery; target precomputation is excluded. The revised text calls this a render–loss–backward benchmark. If retaining “Full update” as the table heading, explain that scope rather than implying end-to-end Adam recovery timing.
6. **Names and typography.** Table I uses “events” while prose and Fig. 4's caption use “notes”; log-TFW2/logTFW2 also differ between assets. These are understandable but could be standardised in a separate figure/table pass. Fig. 1's requested upright component labels are retained; Section II's mathematical component d remains italic with upright t/f subscripts, as previously agreed.
